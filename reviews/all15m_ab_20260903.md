## The capital-efficiency question, answered directly

Baseline ₹-day accounting (capital × days held, per group):

- slow-TF names hold **82.4%** of all ₹-days and earn **₹1.79 per ₹1k-day**
- 15m names hold 17.6% and earn **₹1.51 per ₹1k-day**

The capital parked in long-horizon trades is the **more** productive capital, per rupee per
day, not less. And when the slow blocks are removed, utilisation *drops* (62.3% → 57.1%):
the 15m model at threshold 0.90 simply doesn't fire on most of these names
(RADICO/CHENNPETRO/CUMMINSIND/GESHIP → 0 trades) or fires and loses
(ENGINERSIN −₹11.0k, ADFFOODS −₹18.2k, SCHAEFFLER −₹12.3k, STYLAMIND −₹4.8k).

## The kernel of truth in the hypothesis

Names with **no** override (15m in both runs) do get starved by slow-TF capital today:

- QUESS: 2 trades / −₹3.4k → 114 trades / **+₹28.4k** when the capital is freed
- THANGAMAYL: 2t / +₹1.0k → 52t / +₹16.4k
- TVSMOTOR: 6t / +₹1.8k → 48t / +₹6.2k

So yes — smaller opportunities are being missed (~₹45k of 15m-side upside appears when
capital frees up). But buying that upside by killing the slow book costs **₹228k**. Same
lesson as the falsified slow-TF slot cap and re-entry gates: blocking the biggest winners to
fund marginal entries loses, every time it's been measured.

## Verdict

**All-15m conversion re-FALSIFIED (2nd time, monotone across both runs).** Keep the
aggregated-TF blocks. If the starvation of QUESS-class 15m names is worth chasing, the
lever is funding/execution (todo.md: ledger double-count fix, pre-order margin check) or
more capital — not the timeframe map.
